QKD Lab

Quantum Key
Distribution

This is an interactive lab. Follow along to discover how two people can share a secret cryptographic key using the laws of quantum physics: with security guaranteed by nature itself.

Step 1 · The Sender

Meet Alice

Alice wants to send a confidential message to her friend Bob. Classical encryption alone cannot guarantee perfect security: an eavesdropper could steal the key without leaving any trace. Alice's solution: use individual photons as information carriers.

Step 2 · The Carrier

The Photon

A photon is the fundamental particle of light. Unlike classical bits, a photon cannot be copied or measured without disturbing it.

Each photon also carries a polarization: the orientation of its oscillating electric field. This tiny physical property will become the backbone of the secret key.

Step 3 · Polarization

Four States

A photon can be polarized in four orientations, grouped into two bases. In the rectilinear basis a photon is either vertical (|) or horizontal (—). In the diagonal basis it is tilted at +45° or −45°.

Alice picks a random basis for each photon she sends and encodes a bit (0 or 1) through the chosen orientation. This randomness is what makes the protocol secure.

Step 4 · The Measurement

Polarization Filters

To read a photon, Bob uses a polarization filter tuned to one of the two bases. If he chooses the same basis Alice used, the filter reads the photon correctly and Bob gets the right bit.

If Bob picks the wrong basis, the photon is disturbed and the result is random: he has no way of knowing which answer is correct until Alice tells him afterwards.

Step 5 · The Exchange

Sending a Photon

Alice fires a photon through her filter toward Bob. Toggle Bob's basis and hit Send Photon to see what he reads.

Step 6 · The Receiver

Meet Bob

Bob is waiting at the other end of the quantum channel. For each photon that arrives, he independently picks a random basis (rectilinear or diagonal) to measure it with.

He has no way of knowing which basis Alice used to send it. About half the time he chooses correctly and reads the right bit. The other half gives a useless random result that will be discarded later.

Step 7 · Sifting

Comparing Bases

After all photons are sent, Alice and Bob publicly announce which basis they each used for every photon, but not the actual bits. Where the bases match, the bit is kept. Where they differ, it is discarded.

About half the bits survive. The ones that do form the raw sifted key.

Step 8 · The Eavesdropper

Enter Eve

Eve sits on the quantum channel and intercepts every photon. To read it, she must pick a basis, just like Bob. If she guesses wrong, she disturbs the photon's state before passing it on.

Toggle Eve's basis and press Intercept to see what happens to the photon and what Bob reads.

Step 9 · Error Check

Checking the Channel

Alice and Bob publicly compare a subset of their sifted key bits. If the error rate exceeds ~11%, the channel was compromised and they abort. Below that threshold, the remaining bits are safe to use as the secret key.

Step 10 · The Key

Shared Secret Established

The bits that were neither discarded (wrong basis) nor used for error checking form the final shared secret key. Only Alice and Bob have this key, Eve could not obtain a consistent copy without leaving detectable traces.

This key can now be used in a One-Time Pad or any symmetric cipher to encrypt a message.
